# FSATS V1.4 PROPOSED - Architecture Alignment Design

**Status:** `PART 0 ALIGNMENT BASELINE / OWNER REVIEW REQUIRED`  
**Implementation authority:** `NOT_GRANTED`

## 1. System identity

FSATS is a governed Trading System Boundary / domain grouping inside Falcon OS.

FSATS is not itself a Falcon Application and owns no MSA, Foundation resource allocation, Foundation lifecycle, credentials, hidden persistence or runtime authority as an independent principal.

## 2. Independent Applications

### Inside FSATS operational boundary

1. Falcon Trading Guardian Application.
2. Falcon Self-Aware Provider Management Application (FSAPMA).
3. Falcon Self-Aware Trading Application.

### Adjacent independent Applications preserved from final V1.3

4. Falcon Self-Aware Trading Simulator Application (FSTSimA), independent non-Live validation Application outside FSATS operational authority.
5. Falcon Web Application, independent Shared Application.
6. Falcon Communication Application, independent Shared Application.

Shared and validation Applications are not absorbed into FSATS merely because they support trading workflows.

## 3. Awareness architecture and room locality

Each Falcon Application owns exactly one MSA. Each declared Major Branch owns exactly one LSA. Eligible intelligent components may own one CSA under exactly one parent LSA.

Final V1.3 locality is preserved and aligned to ADR-I015:

- CSA is responsible only for its own eligible component;
- LSA is responsible only for its own Major Branch room and child eligible CSAs;
- MSA understands/supervises only its own Application through declared LSA reports/evidence;
- no rank creates cross-room or cross-Application jurisdiction;
- awareness hierarchy is never a hidden operational integration path;
- all MSA/LSA/CSA entities remain outside the synchronous operational hot path.

## 5. Falcon Trading Guardian Application

Guardian owns trading-domain crisis detection, restriction, protection, open-position safety and controlled recovery.

Binding rules:

- contain incidents at the smallest safe scope;
- where only one user/account is affected, isolate/remediate that scope where safely possible;
- market/system-wide restriction requires evidence of broader trading-domain danger;
- protection commands are scoped, attributable, expiring and contract-governed;
- Guardian cannot enter Trading/FSAPMA internals or direct their MSA/LSA/CSA entities;
- Guardian does not allocate Foundation resources;
- during broad evidenced trading danger, Guardian may request additional technical resources for an affected Application from the Foundation-owned Guardian/resource authority;
- Foundation retains approval, denial, ceilings, prioritization and redistribution.

Canonical Foundation-gap bindings: **FCR-0004 / Issue #4** for protection commands, **FCR-0007 / Issue #7** for resource escalation, **FCR-0009 / Issue #9** for latency-aware transport, and **FCR-0010 / Issue #10** for resource-pressure signals.

## 6. FSAPMA

FSAPMA owns external operational information and provider services required specifically for trading.

Preserved responsibility includes provider registry/capability, onboarding/offboarding, canonical trading-data semantics, provider selection/routing/fallback, external API quota/capacity/free-first knowledge, data quality/freshness/lineage/reconciliation, broker/account capability truth and provider adapters behind owned ports.

FSAPMA does not own Foundation networking, Service Bus, technical-resource allocation, security governance or general Falcon Internet access.

Canonical Foundation-gap bindings include **FCR-0005 / Issue #5** for operational market-data delivery and **FCR-0009 / Issue #9** where the flow is latency-sensitive.

## 7. Operational trading data versus research Internet

Final V1.3 already establishes this boundary and V1.4 preserves it:

- operational external data used in Paper/Live decisions enters through FSAPMA;
- MSA/LSA/eligible CSA Internet access is research, learning, discovery and development only;
- research output cannot directly become operational market truth, a trading signal or an execution command;
- research does not expand room authority or self-authorize adoption;
- candidate improvements must enter governed evidence/testing/review/adoption lifecycle.

Foundation-governed research egress is represented by canonical **FCR-0008 / Issue #8** until a suitable runtime boundary is confirmed available.

## 8. Falcon Self-Aware Trading Application

Trading owns market understanding, analysis, schools, strategies, decision orchestration, business risk, portfolio/capital, execution/positions, trading learning, analytics/attribution and strategy evolution.

Preserved initial V1.3 baseline:

- markets: US Equities and Crypto Spot;
- cash-funded 1:1;
- long-only initial authority;
- 2 trading schools;
- 10 centralized strategy models;
- INTRADAY and SHORT_SWING implementation-eligible horizons;
- MEDIUM_TERM and LONG_TERM disabled future horizons.

Strategies remain centralized in governed registries/controllers. Market profiles provide market-specific constraints/capability inputs and do not duplicate strategy copies.

## 9. Risk and capital

Unified Trading Risk remains Trading-owned business logic.

Preserve hidden-correlation/common-factor/concentration controls, account/custodian/currency-aware capital truth, hierarchical/global capital reservation, same-thesis continuity, aggregate-risk grouping, risk-splitting prevention and protective execution that does not require positive expected profit.

Financial capital is categorically separate from Foundation CPU/RAM/network/storage governance.

## 10. Immutable intent, horizons and execution safety

Preserve final V1.3 hardening: immutable Trading Intent, event-derived lifecycle projections, deterministic horizon clocks/scoped grants, first authoritative non-zero fill timing anchor, no cosmetic horizon reset, dispatch binding identities, dispatch-time authority/Guardian/grant/freshness checks, query/reconcile-before-retry after broker UNKNOWN, reconstructable partial fills/allocation, liquidation obligation without fictional guaranteed close, and non-authoritative replay/test contexts.

## 11. Fast Track and latency architecture

V1.4 explicitly preserves V1.3 Fast Track/performance controls: Data Plane separation, Independent Protection Plane, Fast Risk/feasibility guards, immutable/precomputed snapshots, end-to-end deadline propagation, bounded priority queues, overload/backpressure behavior, p50/p95/p99/p99.9/max evidence, strategy latency classes, load shedding, same-Application colocation where justified and no Risk/Guardian/authority/evidence/reconciliation bypass for speed.

Canonical **FCR-0009 / Issue #9** and **FCR-0010 / Issue #10** carry the confirmed Foundation-facing requirements needed to preserve Fast Track across Application/resource boundaries.

## 12. Independent FSTSimA and Digital City

Final V1.3 defines FSTSimA as an independent non-Live Falcon Application outside FSATS operational authority. This is preserved.

FSTSimA has eight LSA rooms: Simulation Time and Scenario; Market Environment Simulation; Provider and External Service Simulation; Broker/Exchange/Execution Simulation; Account/Capital/Settlement Simulation; Fault/Latency/Crisis Injection; Fidelity and Calibration; Evidence/Oracle/Reproducibility.

FSTSimA uses the same Falcon logic/contracts with simulation-specific adapters, but has no Live credentials/order authority, cannot mutate production state, cannot promote a candidate, cannot approve its own fidelity, cannot weaken Guardian/Risk, and must use separate credentials/stores/namespaces/clocks/authority with enforceable non-Live egress isolation.

Canonical **FCR-0011 / Issue #11** carries the confirmed Foundation enforcement gap. Canonical **FCR-0006 / Issue #6** applies to replay/evidence delivery.

## 13. Shared Web and Communication Applications

Web and Communication remain independent Shared Falcon Applications outside FSATS. FSATS may declare governed presentation/operator-command/notification/report contracts toward them but does not own their authentication, sessions, API gateway, channel delivery, recipient routing, entitlements or communication-security responsibilities.

## 14. Foundation lifecycle versus trading validation/authority

`Sandbox -> FSTSimA -> Digital City -> Live Shadow -> Paper -> Formal Paper Proof -> Tiny Live Calibration -> Controlled Scaling`

An Application may be Foundation `ACTIVE` while having no Paper/Tiny Live/Live trading authority. No Paper, Tiny Live or Live authority is granted by this design.

## 15. Cross-Application interaction

All cross-Application interactions use declared contracts and Foundation-admitted routes when runtime capability exists. Direct hidden coupling is prohibited. Canonical FCR mapping is defined in `08_CROSS_APPLICATION_CONTRACT_MATRIX.md` and `17_PART0_FINAL_FCR_GAP_SCAN.md`.

## 16. Foundation dependency and FCR rule

Confirmed required Foundation gaps use the shared workflow in `applications/FCR_WORKFLOW.md` and repository Issue #1. Canonical FCR identity derives from GitHub Issue number. A pending implementation/empirical/binding value is not automatically an FCR.
