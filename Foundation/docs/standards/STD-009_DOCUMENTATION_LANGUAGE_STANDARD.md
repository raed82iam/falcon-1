# Documentation Language Standard

**Identifier:** STD-009  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Approved  
**Effective Date:** 2026-07-24  
**Approval Record:** GOV-006  
**Owner:** Falcon Standards Authority  
**Governing Authority:** GOV-001; Falcon Constitution Articles 13, 17, 31–32, 37, and 44
**Supersedes:** None  
**Superseded By:** None

## 1. Purpose

This Standard defines language, terminology, and clarity rules for governed Falcon documents.

It protects meaning across time, authors, tools, translations, and implementations. It does not create system behavior or replace document-class Standards.

## 2. Governing Language Rule

Every governed document SHALL declare its authoritative language when more than one language version exists.

A translation is informative unless governance explicitly approves it as an authoritative synchronized version. When authoritative versions differ in meaning, the declared controlling version governs until the conflict is resolved.

Machine-generated or machine-translated text SHALL receive accountable human review before approval when its meaning can create authority or material consequence.

## 3. Normative Terms

Governed English documents SHALL use:

- **SHALL:** mandatory obligation;
- **SHALL NOT:** mandatory prohibition;
- **SHOULD:** recommended practice whose deviation requires reason;
- **SHOULD NOT:** discouraged practice whose use requires reason;
- **MAY:** permitted option; and
- **CAN:** factual capability, not permission.

Lowercase conversational uses shall not create normative authority.

Arabic authoritative documents SHALL define an equivalent controlled vocabulary before approval and SHALL preserve the same obligation strength.

## 4. Requirement Language

Every normative statement SHALL:

- identify the responsible subject;
- express one primary obligation;
- state material condition, scope, and boundary;
- use defined terms consistently;
- be testable, inspectable, or otherwise demonstrable;
- avoid implied exceptions;
- distinguish permission from capability; and
- avoid embedding rationale as an additional hidden obligation.

Ambiguous pronouns SHALL NOT be used where more than one subject is possible.

## 5. Definitions and Terminology

A term with specialized, overloaded, or constitutionally material meaning SHALL be defined in its owning authority.

One concept SHALL have one preferred term within a governed scope. Synonyms SHALL NOT be used when they could imply different owners, states, authorities, or outcomes.

Terms including `Self-Aware`, `authority`, `authorization`, `identity`, `trust`, `risk`, `safe`, `success`, `recovery`, `approval`, `acceptance`, `execution`, `persistence`, `event`, and `state` SHALL be interpreted only through their governing documents.

Self-awareness SHALL NOT be described as consciousness, personhood, unlimited competence, or independent constitutional authority.

## 6. Required Distinctions

Documents SHALL preserve distinctions between:

- fact, observation, interpretation, assumption, and prediction;
- identity, authentication, authorization, and technical access;
- proposal, recommendation, decision, approval, and execution;
- admission, delivery, acceptance, persistence, success, and outcome;
- health, Fitness to Operate, lifecycle state, and Guardian mode;
- failure, degradation, restriction, Safe state, recovery, and normal operation;
- current authoritative state, event, log, evidence, and derived view;
- maintenance, evolution, promotion, and constitutional amendment; and
- technical compatibility, trust, and authority.

## 7. Precision and Uncertainty

Absolute terms such as `always`, `never`, `safe`, `secure`, `complete`, `guaranteed`, `real-time`, `automatic`, `autonomous`, and `fully verified` SHALL be used only when their scope and evidence justify them.

Uncertainty, limitation, dependency, approximation, and non-claim SHALL be stated where material.

Unknown SHALL NOT be described as absent, healthy, zero, successful, or safe.

## 8. Structure and References

Identifiers, versions, statuses, requirement IDs, Contract fields, states, and cross-references SHALL use their canonical form.

A reference SHALL identify enough version or authority context to prevent ambiguity. Links and copied text do not transfer authority.

Normative content SHALL be separated from rationale, examples, notes, open questions, and future ideas.

## 9. Minimality and Readability

Every paragraph SHALL add distinct meaning. Repetition SHALL be removed unless required for independent safety or legal comprehension.

Sentences SHALL prefer direct subjects and verbs. Unnecessary jargon, promotional claims, motivational language, decorative complexity, and unexplained abbreviations are prohibited in governed documents.

Clarity SHALL NOT be achieved by removing necessary conditions, exceptions, uncertainty, ownership, or failure behavior.

## 10. Numbers, Time, and Units

Dates and times SHALL use ISO 8601 with timezone or UTC where time ordering matters.

Quantities SHALL identify units, precision, rounding, currency, reference basis, and effective time where material.

Ranges SHALL state inclusion boundaries. Percentages SHALL identify their denominator. Financial and exact decimal values SHALL not be expressed ambiguously.

## 11. Examples and Diagrams

Examples and diagrams are informative unless explicitly incorporated into a normative statement.

They SHALL NOT contradict governing text, introduce hidden requirements, omit a material safety condition in a misleading way, or be treated as exhaustive unless declared exhaustive.

## 12. Review

Language review SHALL verify:

- semantic consistency with higher authority;
- correct normative strength;
- defined ownership and subjects;
- preserved required distinctions;
- absence of hidden requirements and permissions;
- explicit uncertainty and non-claims;
- canonical identifiers and references;
- translation consistency where applicable; and
- readability by the intended accountable audience.

## 13. Prohibited Practices

Governed documentation SHALL NOT:

- use marketing claims as evidence;
- describe planned behavior as implemented fact;
- use approval, acceptance, verification, or safety interchangeably;
- conceal uncertainty behind precision;
- redefine a canonical term locally without authority;
- use `can` to imply permission;
- silently change normative meaning during formatting or translation; or
- rely on unwritten institutional knowledge for enduring obligations.

## 14. Compatibility and Transition

Approval of this Standard SHALL NOT invalidate an approved document solely because its earlier form lacks a declared language field or uses a legacy expression with clear meaning.

New and materially revised documents SHALL conform. Existing documents SHALL be corrected when ambiguity, translation conflict, terminology drift, or changed meaning is material. Corrections SHALL preserve authority and history under STD-001.

## 15. Acceptance Evidence

Conformance requires:

- declared authoritative language;
- controlled normative vocabulary;
- consistent canonical terminology;
- preserved semantic distinctions;
- precise uncertainty, time, number, and unit language;
- separated normative and informative content;
- accountable language review; and
- no unresolved material ambiguity.
