# Falcon Specifications

Specifications define what must be true.

They contain binding requirements for behavior, boundaries, states, decisions, controls, evidence, and outcomes. They do not exist to describe aspirations, explain code, or preserve meeting history.

## Required Characteristics

Every Specification shall:

- derive authority from the Vision, Constitution, or an approved higher specification;
- have one primary subject and one accountable owner;
- use explicit normative language;
- define scope and non-scope;
- distinguish requirements from rationale;
- state observable acceptance conditions;
- identify dependencies and affected domains;
- declare unresolved matters without presenting them as approved; and
- comply with the Specification Standard.

## Requirement Language

- **SHALL** indicates a binding requirement.
- **SHALL NOT** indicates a binding prohibition.
- **SHOULD** indicates a preferred rule that requires documented justification when not followed.
- **MAY** indicates permitted choice.

Descriptive statements do not create binding requirements unless incorporated by a normative statement.

## What Does Not Belong

A Specification shall not contain:

- philosophical purpose belonging to the Vision;
- constitutional law;
- document-format rules belonging to Standards;
- historical design reasoning belonging to ADRs;
- implementation tasks;
- progress percentages;
- speculative future features; or
- claims of approval without a recorded approval decision.
