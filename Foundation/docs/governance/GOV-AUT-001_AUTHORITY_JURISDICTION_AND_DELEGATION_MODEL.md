# GOV-AUT-001 — Authority Jurisdiction and Delegation Model

**Identifier:** GOV-AUT-001  
**Version:** 1.0  
**Status:** Approved  
**Effective Date:** 2026-07-25  
**Approval Record:** Project Owner approval recorded on 2026-07-25  
**Owner:** Falcon Governance Authority  
**Governing Authority:** Falcon Vision; Falcon Constitution; GOV-000; GOV-001; AUT-001; SEC-002; ADR-I007  
**Affected Domains:** All  
**Implementation Authority:** Not Granted

## 1. Purpose

GOV-AUT-001 defines the legitimate origin, jurisdiction, possession, exercise, delegation, restriction, review, and termination of authority within Falcon.

It prevents technical capability, access, identity, automation, urgency, success, or delegation from being mistaken for lawful authority.

> **Jurisdiction determines where authority may exist. Authority determines who may decide. Permission determines what may be done.**

## 2. Governing Principle

> **Authority SHALL be exercised only within its declared jurisdiction. Delegation SHALL NOT create jurisdiction where none exists.**

Every authority exercised in Falcon SHALL be:

- derived from a valid higher authority;
- held within an established jurisdiction;
- explicit;
- attributable;
- bounded by subject, action, purpose, consequence, environment, and time;
- independently verifiable;
- constrainable;
- reviewable;
- revocable where constitutionally permitted; and
- evidenced.

No authority may expand itself.

## 3. Scope

This document governs:

- creation and recognition of jurisdiction;
- assignment of authority within jurisdiction;
- delegation and redelegation;
- authority chains;
- authority conditions and limits;
- separation of authority;
- conflicts and overlaps;
- temporary and emergency authority;
- suspension, revocation, expiry, and restoration;
- cross-jurisdiction decisions;
- challenges to authority;
- authority evidence; and
- operational verification obligations.

It applies to:

- human authorities;
- automated authorities;
- intelligent or adaptive authorities;
- components;
- external parties acting for Falcon;
- approval bodies;
- protective authorities;
- delegated agents; and
- combined or collegiate decision authorities.

## 4. Non-Scope

GOV-AUT-001 does not:

- create financial authority;
- authorize trading, investment, brokerage, or live-capital activity;
- amend the Vision or Constitution;
- appoint a new constitutional authority;
- replace GOV-000 as the current human authority registry;
- define the internal realization of the Authority Engine;
- define permissions for a specific capability;
- allow Guardian to govern outside its protective mandate;
- treat ownership, access, knowledge, or technical control as authority;
- authorize implementation; or
- validate an authority merely because it is recorded.

## 5. Order of Authority

The controlling order remains the order established by the Constitution and GOV-001.

A lower authority SHALL NOT:

- contradict a higher authority;
- waive a higher obligation;
- redefine the meaning of a higher authority;
- create an exception to a higher authority;
- delegate more authority than it possesses;
- extend a higher authority beyond its jurisdiction; or
- acquire legitimacy through repeated or successful operation.

When authorities conflict, the higher applicable authority prevails. When authorities at the same level conflict, the narrower lawful authority SHALL prevail until the conflict is resolved by the competent governing authority.

## 6. Core Definitions

### 6.1 Jurisdiction

**Jurisdiction** is the formally established domain within which an authority may make specified classes of decisions.

Jurisdiction is defined by:

- subject matter;
- affected resources;
- permitted decision classes;
- prohibited decision classes;
- purpose;
- environment;
- consequence level;
- geographic or legal boundary where applicable;
- temporal boundary;
- governing authority;
- review body; and
- amendment process.

### 6.2 Authority

**Authority** is the governed power assigned to an identified holder to make a defined decision within an existing jurisdiction.

Authority is not:

- identity;
- access;
- technical capability;
- possession;
- expertise;
- responsibility alone;
- influence;
- recommendation;
- prior success; or
- absence of opposition.

### 6.3 Authority Holder

An **Authority Holder** is an identified human, body, role, or governed system to which authority has been validly assigned.

### 6.4 Delegation

**Delegation** is a governed transfer of specified authority from a holder to a delegate without transferring or creating jurisdiction.

The delegator remains accountable for the terms and legitimacy of the delegation unless a higher authority explicitly provides otherwise.

### 6.5 Redelegation

**Redelegation** is a delegation made by a delegate. It is prohibited unless the source delegation explicitly permits it.

### 6.6 Permission

**Permission** is a bounded authorization to perform a defined action. Permission may be issued only by an authority entitled to authorize that action.

### 6.7 Jurisdictional Source

A **Jurisdictional Source** is the higher-authority instrument that lawfully establishes a jurisdiction.

### 6.8 Authority Instrument

An **Authority Instrument** is an immutable governed record that assigns, delegates, restricts, suspends, revokes, or terminates authority.

### 6.9 Authority Chain

An **Authority Chain** is the complete trace from the exercised authority through each assignment or delegation to the valid Jurisdictional Source.

### 6.10 Consequence Class

A **Consequence Class** expresses the maximum material impact an authority may approve or cause. It does not replace domain-specific risk classification.

## 7. Authority Model

The lawful chain is:

```text
Vision
    ↓
Constitution
    ↓
Established Jurisdiction
    ↓
Assigned Authority
    ↓
Valid Delegation, when applicable
    ↓
Verified Permission
    ↓
Governed Action
    ↓
Evidence and Accountability
```

A missing, invalid, expired, conflicted, revoked, or unverifiable link SHALL break the chain.

No decision or action after the broken link acquires authority from the remaining links.

## 8. Establishing Jurisdiction

Jurisdiction SHALL be established only by an authority competent to define that jurisdiction under the controlling governance order.

Every jurisdiction SHALL have a governed charter or equivalent authority record defining:

- Jurisdiction ID;
- title;
- purpose;
- source authority;
- subject matter;
- decision classes;
- exclusions;
- consequence limits;
- applicable environments;
- eligible authority holders;
- appointment authority;
- delegation policy;
- separation-of-duty rules;
- challenge and review path;
- effective date;
- expiry or review date;
- amendment authority; and
- dissolution rule.

Jurisdiction SHALL NOT arise from:

- custom;
- technical necessity;
- urgency;
- operational ownership;
- system access;
- incomplete governance;
- unchallenged behavior;
- successful outcomes;
- prior unauthorized decisions; or
- self-declaration.

## 9. Assigning Authority

Authority assignment SHALL identify:

- Authority ID;
- jurisdiction;
- holder;
- decision classes;
- purpose;
- resources;
- consequence ceiling;
- permitted environments;
- conditions;
- constraints;
- prohibited actions;
- effective time;
- expiry or review condition;
- source authority;
- appointing authority;
- delegation rights;
- revocation authority;
- required independence;
- evidence obligations; and
- challenge path.

Assignment SHALL be accepted by the holder when accountability or active duties are created.

Silence SHALL NOT constitute acceptance of material authority.

## 10. Delegation

A delegation SHALL be narrower than or equal to the delegator's exercisable authority at the time of delegation.

Every delegation SHALL declare:

- Delegation ID;
- delegator;
- delegate;
- source Authority ID;
- jurisdiction;
- delegated decision classes;
- purpose;
- resource scope;
- consequence ceiling;
- conditions;
- constraints;
- effective time;
- expiry;
- revocation rule;
- redelegation rule;
- oversight obligation;
- evidence obligation; and
- acceptance by the delegate where required.

Delegation SHALL NOT:

- create jurisdiction;
- expand the delegator's authority;
- weaken higher obligations;
- transfer non-delegable authority;
- remove delegator accountability;
- bypass required independence;
- hide conflicts of interest;
- convert temporary authority into permanent authority;
- survive expiry or revocation of its source; or
- authorize the delegate to change the rules governing the delegation.

> **No delegated authority acquires jurisdiction beyond that explicitly established by the governing model.**

## 11. Redelegation

Redelegation SHALL be denied by default.

Where expressly permitted, redelegation SHALL:

- remain within the original jurisdiction;
- remain within every upstream constraint;
- be narrower than the immediate delegator's authority;
- preserve the complete Authority Chain;
- preserve accountability at every level;
- terminate no later than any upstream authority;
- remain revocable through every competent upstream authority; and
- identify the final acting delegate.

No number of delegation layers may increase authority.

## 12. Authority Exercise

Before a material decision is exercised, the following SHALL be established:

1. authenticated holder identity;
2. valid jurisdiction;
3. valid authority assignment;
4. valid delegation chain where applicable;
5. requested decision class within scope;
6. legitimate purpose;
7. affected resource within scope;
8. consequence within the permitted ceiling;
9. active conditions satisfied;
10. no applicable prohibition;
11. required independence satisfied;
12. no controlling suspension or protective restriction;
13. current policy and trust state; and
14. sufficient evidence.

Failure to establish any required element SHALL result in denial or bounded restriction.

## 13. Authority Dimensions

Authority SHALL be bounded independently across at least:

- **Action:** what decision or act is permitted;
- **Subject:** what matter is governed;
- **Resource:** what may be affected;
- **Purpose:** why authority may be exercised;
- **Environment:** where it may be exercised;
- **Consequence:** maximum impact;
- **Identity:** who may exercise it;
- **Time:** when and for how long;
- **Frequency:** how often, where relevant;
- **Volume:** how much, where relevant;
- **Dependency:** required approvals or conditions;
- **Evidence:** proof required before and after exercise; and
- **Review:** who may challenge or overturn it.

Permission in one dimension SHALL NOT imply permission in another.

## 14. Authority Classes

Governing documents MAY define authority classes. At minimum, they SHALL distinguish where applicable:

- observation;
- recommendation;
- proposal;
- evaluation;
- validation;
- acceptance;
- approval;
- execution;
- restriction;
- suspension;
- recovery;
- promotion;
- risk acceptance;
- maintenance;
- evolution;
- emergency containment; and
- constitutional change.

The ability to observe, recommend, produce, sign, aggregate, evaluate, or execute SHALL NOT imply authority to approve or accept.

## 15. Separation of Authority

Material decisions SHALL separate incompatible powers according to consequence.

No actor that produces, transforms, aggregates, evaluates, or signs material evidence SHALL be the sole authority that:

- declares the verification case complete;
- accepts its own material Claim;
- approves its own high-consequence change;
- promotes its own artifact;
- resolves a material challenge to its own decision; or
- restores its own unrestricted authority after material compromise.

Where full organizational independence is not yet possible, the limitation SHALL be:

- explicit;
- recorded;
- risk-assessed;
- constrained;
- temporary;
- reviewed by the Project Owner; and
- incapable of bypassing independent technical controls.

## 16. Collegiate and Multi-Party Authority

Where a decision requires more than one authority, the governing instrument SHALL define:

- required roles;
- quorum;
- unanimity or voting rule;
- independence conditions;
- conflict-of-interest rules;
- abstention;
- substitution;
- timeout;
- deadlock resolution;
- dissent preservation; and
- final accountable authority.

Combining several insufficient authorities SHALL NOT create sufficient authority unless an Approved governing instrument explicitly creates a collegiate authority within established jurisdiction.

## 17. Cross-Jurisdiction Decisions

A decision affecting multiple jurisdictions SHALL require one of:

- separate valid approval from each affected jurisdiction;
- an Approved joint authority established over the intersection; or
- escalation to a higher authority competent over all affected jurisdictions.

One jurisdiction SHALL NOT unilaterally consume, override, or reinterpret another jurisdiction's authority.

Where jurisdictions conflict or their boundary is uncertain, the action SHALL be restricted pending authoritative resolution.

## 18. Conflict of Interest

Material conflicts of interest SHALL be disclosed before authority is exercised.

Governing policy SHALL determine whether the conflict requires:

- recusal;
- independent review;
- additional approval;
- reduced authority;
- replacement of the holder; or
- denial.

An undisclosed material conflict SHALL make the affected decision challengeable and MAY suspend reliance on it.

## 19. Automated and Intelligent Authority

Automated or intelligent authority SHALL be:

- explicitly assigned;
- limited by action and consequence class;
- constrained by demonstrated competence;
- tied to current evidence and fitness;
- interruptible;
- revocable;
- observable;
- independently testable; and
- incapable of self-expansion.

Learning, prediction, optimization, repeated success, apparent confidence, or generated policy SHALL NOT create authority.

An automated authority SHALL NOT:

- appoint itself;
- delegate unless explicitly permitted;
- broaden its jurisdiction;
- alter its own authority source;
- approve its own material evolution;
- weaken Guardian;
- conceal its uncertainty; or
- convert experimental authority into permanent authority.

## 20. Guardian and Protective Authority

Guardian holds protective authority only as granted by the Constitution and Approved Specifications.

Protective authority MAY:

- deny;
- restrict;
- suspend;
- isolate;
- require Safe Mode;
- preserve evidence;
- initiate escalation; and
- prevent an increase in exposure.

Protective authority SHALL NOT be interpreted as general authority to:

- create policy;
- authorize financial action;
- approve profit-seeking behavior;
- erase evidence;
- expand Guardian's own mandate; or
- restore unrestricted authority without the required independent confirmation.

When valid protective authority conflicts with a permissive subordinate authority, the protective restriction prevails until lawfully resolved.

## 21. Emergency Authority

Emergency authority exists only to contain imminent material harm within an established emergency jurisdiction.

Emergency authority SHALL:

- have an explicit trigger;
- use the minimum necessary power;
- preserve non-waivable obligations;
- prefer reversible action;
- be time-bounded;
- produce immediate evidence;
- notify the designated oversight authority;
- prohibit silent renewal;
- require post-action review; and
- terminate when the emergency condition ends.

Emergency action SHALL NOT:

- create enduring jurisdiction;
- create permanent authority;
- amend higher authority;
- conceal evidence;
- normalize an exception;
- authorize unrelated action; or
- become precedent merely through success.

## 22. Suspension, Revocation, Expiry, and Termination

Authority SHALL cease to be exercisable when:

- its purpose ends;
- its time expires;
- its source expires or is revoked;
- its holder loses eligibility;
- required conditions fail;
- required trust cannot be established;
- a controlling protective restriction applies;
- jurisdiction is dissolved;
- governing policy changes incompatibly; or
- competent authority suspends or revokes it.

Suspension temporarily prevents exercise without erasing the authority record.

Revocation withdraws authority according to the governing instrument.

Expiry ends authority automatically at a defined boundary.

Termination closes authority because its purpose, jurisdiction, or holder relationship has ended.

Historical authority evidence SHALL remain preserved.

## 23. Restoration of Authority

Restoration SHALL NOT occur solely because the original failure is no longer visible.

Restoration requires:

- identification of the affected authority;
- evidence of cause and scope;
- resolution or containment of the triggering condition;
- verification of identity, integrity, policy, and current conditions;
- assessment of residual uncertainty;
- confirmation by the required independent authority;
- a new restoration decision;
- explicit restored scope; and
- preserved evidence.

The authority whose conduct or trust is under review SHALL NOT be the sole authority that restores itself in a material case.

Restored authority MAY be narrower than the original authority.

## 24. Challenge and Review

Every material Authority Instrument and authority decision SHALL have a challenge path.

A Challenge SHALL identify:

- challenged authority or decision;
- grounds;
- challenger;
- jurisdiction;
- consequence;
- evidence;
- requested protection;
- reviewing authority;
- independence requirement; and
- resolution.

A challenged authority SHALL NOT conclusively validate itself in a material dispute.

Low-impact self-correction MAY be allowed only when explicitly defined by governing policy and independently auditable.

## 25. Authority Evidence

Authority evidence SHALL preserve:

- identity of the jurisdiction;
- Jurisdictional Source;
- Authority Instrument;
- complete Authority Chain;
- holder identity;
- delegation and redelegation;
- active policy versions;
- conditions and constraints;
- consequence class;
- requested and decided action;
- decision basis;
- decision time;
- expiry;
- outcome;
- denials and reasons;
- suspensions and revocations;
- conflicts and challenges;
- responsible authority; and
- integrity protection.

Authority evidence is a Trust Object governed by SEC-002.

Missing evidence SHALL NOT be interpreted as evidence of permission.

## 26. Authority States

An Authority Instrument SHALL use an Approved lifecycle. The common governance states are:

- `DRAFT`;
- `PENDING_ACCEPTANCE`;
- `ACTIVE`;
- `RESTRICTED`;
- `SUSPENDED`;
- `EXPIRED`;
- `REVOKED`;
- `TERMINATED`; and
- `ARCHIVED`.

Only `ACTIVE` authority may grant its full declared permissions.

`RESTRICTED` authority may grant only the explicitly preserved subset.

No other state grants operative authority.

## 27. Default-Deny Rules

Authority SHALL be denied when:

- jurisdiction is missing or uncertain;
- source authority is unverified;
- the holder is unauthenticated;
- the action is outside the declared decision class;
- the purpose is absent or invalid;
- consequence exceeds the ceiling;
- a required condition is unproven;
- a prohibition applies;
- delegation is invalid;
- redelegation is not permitted;
- independence is unsatisfied;
- authority is not `ACTIVE` or applicable `RESTRICTED`;
- authority evidence is materially incomplete;
- a higher restriction applies; or
- the governing model cannot reach a determinate result.

Uncertainty about authority SHALL reduce authority.

## 28. Governance Requirements

- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-001:** Every exercised authority SHALL trace to an established jurisdiction.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-002:** Every jurisdiction SHALL trace to a competent higher authority.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-003:** No authority holder SHALL exercise authority outside the holder's declared jurisdiction.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-004:** Delegation SHALL NOT create, widen, transfer, or reinterpret jurisdiction.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-005:** A delegate SHALL receive no greater authority than the delegator can lawfully exercise.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-006:** Redelegation SHALL be denied unless expressly authorized.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-007:** Every authority chain SHALL preserve all upstream constraints.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-008:** Authority SHALL be bounded by purpose, scope, consequence, environment, identity, time, and conditions.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-009:** Technical capability, access, possession, expertise, recommendation, or success SHALL NOT establish authority.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-010:** Missing or materially uncertain authority SHALL result in denial or restriction.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-011:** A lower authority SHALL NOT waive a higher obligation.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-012:** Separation of authority SHALL be proportionate to consequence.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-013:** Material authority SHALL be constrainable, interruptible, reviewable, and revocable where constitutionally permitted.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-014:** Automated and intelligent authorities SHALL NOT expand or approve their own authority.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-015:** Emergency authority SHALL be minimal, temporary, evidenced, and incapable of creating enduring authority.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-016:** Restoration of material authority SHALL require evidence and the required independent confirmation.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-017:** Every material authority decision SHALL be attributable and reconstructable.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-018:** Challenges to material authority SHALL have an independent resolution path.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-019:** Authority evidence SHALL be governed as Trust Objects under SEC-002.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-020:** Delegation, suspension, revocation, expiry, and termination SHALL propagate to dependent authority according to the governing instrument.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-021:** An expired, suspended, revoked, terminated, conflicted, or unverifiable authority SHALL NOT grant permission.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-022:** Cross-jurisdiction decisions SHALL require authority competent across every affected jurisdiction.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-023:** Material conflicts of interest SHALL be disclosed and governed before authority is exercised.
- **GOV-AUT-001-REQ-024:** No delegated authority SHALL acquire jurisdiction beyond that explicitly established by the governing model.

## 30. Conformance Evidence

Conformance requires evidence that:

- authority without jurisdiction is denied;
- delegation cannot create or widen jurisdiction;
- a delegate cannot exceed the delegator;
- invalid redelegation is denied;
- upstream expiry and revocation constrain downstream authority;
- cross-jurisdiction action cannot use one jurisdiction as a substitute for another;
- technical access does not grant permission;
- conflicting and uncertain authority causes restriction;
- separation-of-duty constraints cannot be self-waived;
- automated authority cannot expand itself;
- Guardian restriction overrides conflicting subordinate permission;
- emergency power expires and cannot silently renew;
- restoration requires independent evidence;
- challenged material authority cannot resolve itself conclusively;
- every decision preserves its complete Authority Chain; and
- authority evidence conforms to SEC-002.

## 32. Foundational Rules

> **No jurisdiction, no authority.**

> **No authority, no permission.**

> **No verified chain, no exercise.**

> **Delegation transfers bounded authority; it does not create jurisdiction.**

> **Capability is not permission. Success is not legitimacy. Silence is not approval.**

> **Jurisdiction → Authority → Acceptance → Reliance.**
