# AMD-004 Constitutional Compliance Report

**Status:** Acknowledged and Approved  
**Approval Record:** GOV-061  
**Review Date:** 2026-07-27  
**Constitutional Amendment:** Not Required

## 1. Result

No direct constitutional conflict was found.

The proposed FSA → MSA → LSA → CSA architecture is a subordinate allocation of awareness responsibilities. It does not alter Falcon’s purpose, Prime Objective, constitutional identity, authority hierarchy, or reserved approval.

## 2. Evidence

### Constitutional Identity and Self-Awareness

Constitution Article 6 requires Falcon to maintain evidence-based understanding of its financial, operational, decisional, epistemic, and authority condition sufficient to govern fitness.

The hierarchy preserves this obligation collectively:

- FSA understands Foundation condition;
- MSA understands Applications ecosystem condition;
- LSA understands Application and business-domain condition;
- CSA understands eligible component condition.

The Constitution does not require one Foundation component to own every category.

### Separation of Responsibilities

The proposal strengthens separation by preventing FSA from owning Application business meaning and by preserving distinct FSA, MSA, LSA, CSA, Guardian, Authority Engine, Security, Risk, and governance roles.

### Bounded Authority

The proposal states that:

- awareness does not create jurisdiction;
- FSA conformance is scoped;
- FSA cannot amend the Constitution;
- FSA cannot approve its own authority expansion;
- Guardian and Authority Engine retain their respective authority;
- business and financial approvals remain with competent owners.

This is consistent with the Constitution’s bounded-authority and independent-control obligations.

### Capital Protection

Removing financial detail from FSA does not remove financial awareness from Falcon.

Financial meaning remains with MSA, LSA, CSA where eligible, CAP, FIN, RSK, and competent Application/domain authorities. FSA receives technical criticality and abstract impact sufficient to protect Foundation and escalate to Guardian or other authorities.

This preserves protection without creating Foundation business coupling.

### Evidence and Historical Integrity

The package preserves AWR-001 v1.0 unchanged, proposes versioned successors, preserves approval history, and requires attributable evidence, challenge, reconstruction, correction, and supersession.

### Safe Failure

Unknown, stale, contradictory, integrity-failed, or unavailable awareness cannot become healthy, fit, ready, or conforming. FSA compromise requires isolation and independent recovery.

## 3. Owner Review Required

Owner review remains required because the proposal:

- changes the approved architecture meaning of AWR-001;
- establishes FSA conformance jurisdiction;
- reserves new specification identifiers;
- introduces new awareness authorities;
- proposes eventual supersession of AWR-001 v1.0.

Owner review is an approval requirement, not evidence of constitutional conflict.

## 4. Constitutional Amendment Decision

**No constitutional amendment is required.**

No constitutional text shall be modified as part of AMD-004.

## 5. Self-Repair and Self-Evolution Extension

The extension remains constitutionally compatible because:

- Self-Repair restores only a previously Approved trusted state;
- Self-Evolution creates only a non-authoritative isolated candidate;
- independent validation and Owner approval separate creation from admission;
- FSA cannot approve its own candidate or authority expansion;
- deployment remains separately authorized;
- rollback returns to an Approved trusted state;
- Guardian mandate, Authority Engine jurisdiction, Security, evidence, and governance remain independent;
- Application business logic and state remain outside FSA scope;
- unknown and incomplete evidence cannot become approval.

The extension operationalizes governed maintenance and evolution without changing Falcon’s constitutional purpose or authority hierarchy.
