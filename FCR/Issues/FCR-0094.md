### 1. Application-owned business/domain boundary

The current FSATS boundary contains five independent Falcon Applications:

1. Falcon Self-Aware Trading Application;
2. FSAPMA;
3. Falcon Trading Guardian Application;
4. FSTSimA;
5. APP-RSC / Falcon Self-Aware Resource Management Application.

FSATS itself is not an Application/runtime principal and owns no independent business authority.

Each Application retains ownership of its own business/domain state, decisions, commands, workflows, outcomes, and domain meaning. In particular:

- Trading owns trading/strategy/risk/portfolio/execution business semantics inside its accepted scope;
- FSAPMA owns provider-management and operational-data-provider business semantics inside its accepted scope;
- Trading Guardian owns its accepted trading-protection domain semantics and outcomes;
- FSTSimA owns simulation/test-market semantics inside its accepted scope;
- APP-RSC owns FSATS-only aggregate resource-coordination semantics inside its accepted scope, but it does **not** own Foundation total-resource truth, grants, ceilings, floors, or Foundation Resource Governance.

Shared Web may present or relay Application-emitted state, evidence, alerts, decisions, requests and outcomes through governed contracts without acquiring ownership of those semantics.

Shared Web SHALL NOT infer, decide, rewrite, mint, mutate or execute Application business meaning merely because it presents the information or transports a request. It SHALL NOT acquire direct access to Application-private databases, memory, credentials, internal components or undeclared routes.

Generic Falcon-wide rule:

`PRESENTATION_OR_TRANSPORT != BUSINESS_OWNERSHIP != AUTHORITY != EXECUTION`

Generic cross-Application interaction must use declared governed contracts/routes. FSATS-specific payload meanings, trading stages, Guardian semantics, APP-RSC semantics, provider/broker meanings and the P1-K family graph remain Application-owned.

### 2. Application-to-Shared-Web interaction model

The current accepted Application-side model supports the following governed interaction categories where the relevant family exists:

- **Authoritative Application state projection:** the owning Application produces the authoritative domain state/projection; Web may present it but does not become the source of truth.
- **Informational query/response:** Web may relay a governed query; the owning Application returns the authoritative response.
- **Authenticated Owner request:** Web may act as a presentation/request transport for an attributable Owner request. Delivery of the request does not mean acceptance or execution.
- **Application acceptance/rejection/outcome:** the owning Application evaluates the request under its own domain and authority rules and returns an explicit governed result/evidence.
- **Event/notification projection:** Application-owned events/alerts may be projected through governed communication boundaries where declared.

P1-K requires governed families to preserve exact producer/consumer identity, direction, purpose/business meaning, authority/security class, schema identity/version, FIL/route binding where applicable, correlation/causation/idempotency, time/expiry/deadline semantics, ordering/duplicate/correction rules, replay/test/operational classification, acceptance/rejection rules, dependencies and positive/negative fixtures.

For APP-RSC/resource-related flows, current Part 2 compatibility evidence additionally verifies fail-closed treatment of stale/mismatched resource epochs, projection identity mismatch, request/outcome/correlation mismatch and partial/denied outcomes. APP-RSC cannot mint Foundation resource authority.

For Shared Web control/request semantics, the mandatory invariant remains:

`REQUEST_SENT != ACTION_ACCEPTED != ACTION_COMPLETED`

A transport acknowledgement cannot be represented as an Application acceptance, and an Application acceptance cannot be represented as completed action unless authoritative completion evidence exists.

The interaction **pattern** may be reusable across Falcon Applications. The payload meaning, business decision, acceptance criteria and business outcome remain owned by the producing/receiving Application unless a separately accepted generic contract explicitly defines otherwise.

### 4. Application truth and failure semantics exposed to Web

Shared Web must preserve the following Application-side truth distinctions:

- **authoritative state:** emitted by the owning Application through a governed contract/evidence path;
- **derived/presented state:** a Web rendering or aggregation, not a replacement authoritative source;
- **stale:** explicitly stale and not silently promoted to current;
- **unknown:** explicitly unknown and not converted into false/zero/safe/allowed;
- **unavailable:** source unavailable is distinct from a valid empty/healthy state;
- **contradictory:** material conflicting evidence remains visible/fail-closed according to the governing family rather than being silently reconciled by Web;
- **partial visibility:** partial evidence remains marked partial;
- **pending:** request/action remains pending until authoritative disposition exists;
- **rejected:** distinct from failed transport and distinct from accepted;
- **accepted:** distinct from completed;
- **completed:** requires authoritative completion/outcome evidence;
- **failed:** must remain a failure and preserve relevant reason/evidence;
- **replay/historical:** clearly identified as replay/historical and never presented as current operational truth.

Where the governing contract requires them, Web must preserve correlation, causation, request identity, outcome identity, evidence identity, epoch/fencing identity, timestamps, expiry/deadline and replay/idempotency semantics end to end.

No raw provider/broker credential bytes or Application-private account internals become reusable Web/browser state merely for presentation convenience.

Existing primary reconciliation reference: FCR-0077, including the current accepted rule that stale/unknown/unavailable remain explicit and `REQUEST_SENT != ACTION_ACCEPTED != ACTION_COMPLETED`.

### 5. Authority and control boundary

Shared Web does not become an Application permission or business-decision authority by exposing a control surface.

For an authenticated/privileged Owner request, the Application-side expectation is that the governed boundary preserves enough attributable authority context to evaluate the request, including as applicable:

- exact requester/Owner identity;
- exact target Application/principal;
- request identity;
- authority/delegation/security context;
- scope and requested action;
- correlation/causation;
- freshness/expiry/deadline;
- relevant epoch/fencing state;
- required evidence/provenance.

The receiving Application remains responsible for its Application-owned acceptance/rejection/business outcome. A stale, replayed, expired, mismatched, unauthorized or otherwise invalid request must fail closed according to the governing contract.

The Application must return an explicit authoritative disposition/outcome rather than allowing Web to infer success from successful transport.

For current FSATS emergency/control planning, P1-K/FCR-0077 already preserve Guardian protection command/outcome semantics, APP-RSC coordination epoch/fencing state, AI containment/Kill state and Controlled Revival evidence. Shared Web remains a presentation/request surface only.

Authority split remains:

- **Foundation:** OS/application lifecycle, admission, generic security/governance, Foundation communication rules, total-resource governance, Foundation/FSA jurisdiction and other Foundation-owned platform authority;
- **Application:** its business/domain decisions, internal business state, domain command acceptance/rejection, domain outcomes and internal business recovery;
- **Shared Web:** presentation and governed request/interaction surface within its own approved Application boundary;
- **Owner/governance:** explicit higher-order approvals/adoption/authority decisions where Falcon governance requires them.

No current Part 2 implementation authority grants runtime route activation, external connectivity, Provider/Broker access, Paper, Shadow, Tiny-Live, Live, deployment or production adoption.
